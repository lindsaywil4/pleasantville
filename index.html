Choke points
Six places where a stopped train cuts Pleasantville off. Tap one to see it on the map.
The bigger squeeze. Englewood Yard, 2 miles west, is the yard UP’s own merger screening (Doc. 311221 App. Q) shows growing 20.1 percent in carloads, crossing the federal environmental review threshold for this nonattainment area. The Lafayette corridor 3 miles north gains 5 trains a day. More yard volume means more switching on the Market Street lead that forms Pleasantville’s northern boundary, and more port trains on the PTRA line along Clinton Drive to the south. The neighborhood sits between the two.
Data caveat. Through-train counts shown are UP’s own FRA inventory filings, which this project has documented as stale at several East Houston crossings. PTRA crossings along Clinton Dr are reported separately by PTRA and are not shown with counts here.
